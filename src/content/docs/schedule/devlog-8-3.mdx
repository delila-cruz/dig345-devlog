1. Functionality
The prototype proves that the conversation/chat aspect of the game will work. The simulation of a chat enviromnent is the most crucial part of the project, since the whole idea is that it's a chatbot simulation.
2. Visual
![Video](prototype.mp4)
3. Separation of Concerns
The idea of separation of concerns is demonstrated in my project by my use of Twine. It both saves me time I'd spend trying to figure out how to code something like this, and it's a tried-and-true way to create this sort of story format. If I tried to do it myself, it would probably have much more non-essential code than it needed, especially when it's first starting out.
4. What did you struggle with to finish the proof of concept?
It took me a while to figure out which Twine story format was best for this project. I talked to Prof. Sample about Twine and he gave me an example of a piece that he did that used Trialogue, which created the chat effect I was looking for. After that, I just worked on reading the docs and figuring out how to use it.
5. Most helpful resource
Trialogue Docs: https://phivk.github.io/trialogue/
6. 1st most important feature
The 1st most important feature I will implement is the ChatGPT-inspired styling. This creates the familiar but unsettling effect I'm hoping for.
7. 2nd most important feature
The 2nd most important feature I will implement is the ability to change the name of "You" and "Bot." I think it's more eerie to see your name and the deceased's name side-by-side. I will do this by adding a "log-in" page.
8. 3rd most important feature
The 3rd most important feature I will add is the About page. I think this game works on its own without explanation but it could be nice to include anyway, especially since the first page is a Log-In that doesn't tell you much about the story (the about link will go underneath)
